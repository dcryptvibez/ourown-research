A2. Distribution structure

Chain: manufacturer/importer → primary distributor → market wholesaler → kiosk/provision store → consumer (modern trade bypasses the middle layers). Shares of FMCG volume: open market 78–85%, kiosks 8–15%, modern trade 2–8%, e-commerce <1% (DALA/NielsenIQ 2026 [Medium]). USDA FAS Retail Foods Annual Nigeria (NI2024-0014, 3 Oct 2024): ~70% of food shopping in informal channels; open-air markets 72% of retail sales, convenience/small grocery 25% [snippet — PDF could not be opened; verify manually, it is the most citable set]. Euromonitor: 40–90% of food sales informal across sub-Saharan Africa (2022) [High].

Multi-layer routing costs the brand 30–40% of unit margin across layers (DALA); open-market trade is cash-based, distributor trade runs on 30–90-day cycles — i.e. direct buying costs more capital but less per unit, or vice versa (see Area E).

A3. Category & basket drivers

  • Food = 56.65% of household expenditure (NBS Consumption Expenditure Pattern 2019, NLSS 2018/19) [High]; largest components: food consumed outside home, transport, starchy roots/tubers — combined 24.16%.
  • Derived 2019 household food spend ≈ ₦47,500/month (2019 naira, own arithmetic) — escalate with CPI before use; do not quote as a 2026 number.
  • Fresh food = 52% of Nigerian online grocery revenue (ECDB 2025).

A5. Addressable vs total

Total grocery expenditure ≠ addressable. Online grocery today is US$95m nationally. Any OurOwn number must be built bottom-up (Deliverable 3), never as "X% of Nigeria's grocery market".

A6. Why the North differs from Lagos

  • Modern trade: Lagos >1,400 supermarkets; Abuja #2 (higher basket); "outside these three cities, modern trade penetration drops sharply; distribution relies on wholesale and informal route-to-market" — Kano listed only as an emerging modern-trade city (DALA 2026) [read].
  • Poverty: North West has Nigeria's highest poverty headcount; Kano State MPI 66.3% (NBS/OPHI 2022, snippet-triangulated) with the largest absolute poor population of any state.
  • Food insecurity epicentre: Cadre Harmonisé — 27.2m in Crisis or worse Oct–Dec 2025, projected 34.7m Jun–Aug 2026, Sahel/North-West concentrated.
  • Counterweight: Kano is a supply hub (Dawanau) — sourcing advantage, but also means customers know wholesale prices and will compare them.

Research Area F — Business model & unit economics

Full workbook spec in 06-unit-economics-workbook-spec.md; the numbers below are hypothetical inputs to be replaced with quotations.

Definitions: Sales revenue − COGS = gross profit. Gross profit − (payment + packaging + delivery + discounts + losses) = contribution. Contribution − fixed opex = operating profit. Cash flow ≠ profit (inventory prepayment + T+1 settlement means cash lags sales).

Base hypothetical inputs: payment = 1.5% + ₦100 (Paystack local rate, cap ₦2,000 — verified published fee); packaging ₦250; delivery ₦1,200/order (single-drop) or ₦600 (batched); losses/spoilage/returns 2% of revenue; no discount in base case.

Contribution per order (₦, single-drop delivery ₦1,200, no delivery fee collected):

Basket 5% 8% 10% 12% 15%
5,000 −1,475 −1,325 −1,225 −1,125 −975
10,000 −1,400 −1,100 −900 −700 −400
20,000 −1,250 −650 −250 +150 +750
30,000 −1,100 −200 +400 +1,000 +1,900

With a ₦1,000 delivery fee collected: ₦5,000 basket still negative at every margin ≤12%; ₦10,000 turns positive at 10% (+₦100); ₦20,000 positive at 8% (+₦350); ₦30,000 positive at 8% (+₦800).

Batched delivery (₦600/order) + ₦800 fee: every basket ≥₦5,000 is ≥ breakeven at 5–8% — batching is the single biggest structural lever.

Conclusions: 1. 5% gross margin alone cannot support the business unless baskets are ≥₦20,000, delivery is batched, and a fee is charged — and even then fixed costs are unfunded (contribution ₦150–750/order). 2. To cover an illustrative ₦750,000/month fixed cost (2 staff, small store, tools, minimal marketing) you need ~1,000–3,000 contribution-positive orders/month depending on scenario — i.e. ₦10m–₦27m monthly sales. This is the real bar. 3. Free delivery funded inside price: recovering ₦1,200 inside a ₦10,000 basket = +12% price — OurOwn would be visibly more expensive than the market → conversion loss + trust loss. Do not embed delivery undisclosed. 4. Category differentiation is mandatory: staples/oils/confectionery can hold 8–12%; branded FMCG (price-transparent) may only hold 3–6%; fresh produce spoilage argues for phase 2.

Research Area G — Sales targets (₦60m / ₦100m)

Orders required (own arithmetic):

AOV Orders/yr ₦60m Orders/mo per day (365d) per day (300 op. days) Orders/yr ₦100m per day (300d)
5,000 12,000 1,000 33 40 20,000 67
10,000 6,000 500 16 20 10,000 33
15,000 4,000 333 11 13 6,667 22
20,000 3,000 250 8 10 5,000 17

At 10% gross margin, ₦60m = ₦6m gross profit and ₦100m = ₦10m — before payment, packaging, delivery, losses, staff, rent, tech, marketing (see Area F: variable costs alone consume much of this). ₦100m of sales is not ₦100m of profit; at the modelled blended contribution it is plausibly ₦3m–₦8m of contribution, i.e. roughly breakeven to modestly positive at fixed cost ₦750k–₦1m/month.

Assessment: ₦60m (≈ ₦5m/month) is demanding but constructible: at ₦10k AOV it is 500 orders/month ≈ 17/day. ₦100m needs 833 orders/month ≈ 28/day — that is a real operation requiring proven repeat purchase. Both targets are inconsistent with launch month one; they are year-1-to-2 targets conditional on repeat-purchase rates nobody has measured yet. Recommend re-basing targets on orders/day, AOV, repeat rate and contribution/order — revenue follows.

Research Area H — Delivery & fulfilment

Full analysis in Deliverables 6 and 11; evidence highlights:

  • Customer-facing fees: entry band ₦1,400–₦1,700 (Jumia vendor/waybill rates, Lagos intra-city ₦1,400 small parcel — snippet); Lagos last-mile ≈ ₦2,970 (Fez, snippet); Kwik Lagos intra-hop ₦1,200 (Kwikpik blog, Nov 2025); Glovo Prime ₦1,999/mo subscription; Chowpass free delivery over ₦3,000 (₦3,500/mo). The founders' ₦1,500–₦1,700 Kano figure sits inside the observed Nigerian band — plausible, but zero Kano-specific data exists (the founders' number and the market's number have not been tested against each other).
  • Rider economics: employed riders ₦60k–₦130k/mo (2026, low-authority aggregator — order of magnitude only); independent Lagos riders ₦10k–₦25k/day; bike rental ~₦10k/day [trade chatter, unverified]; fuel ₦2,000–₦3,500/day → variable floor ≈ ₦350–₦600 per drop before rider margin → single-drop pricing below ~₦700–₦1,000 is loss-making; hence industry batching.
  • 3PLs with Kano evidence: GIG Logistics — 3 physical centres in Kano (Bompai Rd; Centro Plaza opposite BUK Old Site; Zaria Rd) but closed Sundays; Fez Delivery — Kano hub launched 2026 (snippet; JS page) ; Jumia Delivery waybill 100+ cities; Kwik has retreated to Lagos-only per its own site (important negative); local dispatchers (Safiyya, CC Delivery, Flexy Movers, Danhausa, CETRAH) — no published tariffs.
  • COD/failed delivery: COD ≈ 23% of online sales (broader "pay on delivery" up to ~45%); rejection 20–25% for SME sellers, fashion >30% (Selligate — a COD vendor's own page, no primary citation — use as planning assumption, not fact). Chowdeck deliberately discouraged COD for new riders (TechCabal, 2023) [High]. A refused perishable order is a write-off.
  • Cold chain: Nigeria needs ~25,000 reefer trucks, has <1,000 (USITA, Mar 2022); >40% fresh produce post-harvest loss (widely repeated, sponsor-content provenance). Phase 1 must be ambient-first; chilled only with owned cold storage.

Research Area J — Technology, security & product readiness

Full checklist in 08-technical-operational-readiness.md. Evidence highlights:

  • No Nigerian outage has a published ₦ cost — instrument your own. Paystack publishes per-component uptime (API 99.97%, Checkout 100%, some components 97.4% over 90 days) and channel-specific maintenance notices (Interswitch maintenance degrades Verve cards only) → build per-channel health/fallback, one "global payments up/down" flag is wrong.
  • Network reality: Nigeria mobile median ~43–90 Mbps (two views on Ookla's page, Sept 2026) but design for the 10th percentile: <1Mbps, 300–800ms RTT; Android 84.2% share, Tecno/Infinix dominant → low-RAM, small-screen, Android 8–10 target (StatCounter Sept 2026).
  • Regulation: CBN Risk-Based Cybersecurity Framework (31 May 2024, effective 1 Jul 2024) binds banks/PSPs and cascades to merchants by contract (24-hour incident reporting, PCI-DSS/ISO27001 for third parties); NDPA + GAID 2025 (audit returns due 31 March via licensed DPCO; penalties up to ₦10m or 2% of gross revenue); no "Cybersecurity Act 2025" or "NCSA" exists — the operative law is the Cybercrimes Act 2015 as amended 2024, and its 0.5% levy was suspended (May 2024) — do not build a levy line item.
  • PCI: hosted checkout only → SAQ A likely scope (v4.0.1 tightened eligibility 1 Apr 2025 — confirm with the PSP in writing). Never store card data.
  • Prototype ≠ production: founder-reported features (Deliverable brief §2.5) are unverified; no code audit, security test or payment-settlement test exists. What must be tested directly is listed in Deliverable 8 §4.

Research Area K — Legal, regulatory, tax & consumer obligations

Full memo in 09-regulatory-compliance-memo.md (verification date 30 Oct 2026). Highlights:

  • FIRS no longer exists: it is now the Nigeria Revenue Service (NRS) under the 2025 tax-reform package (nrs.gov.ng) — CIT 0% for small companies (turnover ≤₦100m, fixed assets ≤₦250m), 30% otherwise; Development Levy 4% (except small companies); VAT 7.5% with basic food items zero-rated — a grocery basket is partially zero-rated; do not charge 7.5% flat.
  • FCCPC: e-commerce guidance requires clear pre-payment disclosure of all costs including delivery, legible T&Cs + privacy policy, complaint redress mechanism; "misleading pricing, weak complaints handling, unlabelled goods" are the live enforcement themes. Draft Consumer Protection Regulations 2026 (comments closed 4 May 2026) contain online-platform duties (incl. no review manipulation) — draft, not yet law.
  • NDPA 2023/NDPC: OurOwn will almost certainly cross the "200 data subjects in 6 months" registration threshold after launch; consent for marketing/location sharing with riders; breach notification; annual audit returns at higher tiers. Exact threshold/72-hour notice figures are [Unverified — primary PDFs not machine-readable].
  • NAFDAC: reselling registered packaged goods generally needs no product registration — but repacking rice/beans into own bags makes OurOwn a manufacturer/packer (full registration + labelling + food-handler regime). This is the single most important NAFDAC question for a Kano dark store.
  • Riders: NICN declined to find Uber drivers employees (Olatunji v Uber, 2017) — contractor status turns on control, not labels; scheduled/uniformed/exclusive riders risk reclassification → PAYE, pension, ECA, dismissal exposure.
  • There is no "Kano Emira" levy and no Nigerian "Electronic Transactions Act 2015" as far as official sources show — both brief items could not be verified; ask the lawyer.
