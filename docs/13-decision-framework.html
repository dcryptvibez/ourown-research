Deliverable 13 — Decision Framework: Direct Answers to the Founders' 17 Questions

Rule applied: every answer states the reasoning, the supporting evidence, the uncertainty, and the next action. Where evidence was insufficient, the answer is a concrete test rather than an opinion.

1. Should OurOwn launch first in Kano, and why? Yes. Kano has no delivery incumbent (Glovo /kano → 404; PricePally/Chowdeck absent; Jumia Food and Bolt Food dead nationally), it is a top-3 connectivity market (9.03m internet subs), and it is the founders' home advantage plus the sourcing hub (Dawanau, Singer, Ghadco, Mamuda, NBC Challawa) — a landed-cost edge no Lagos competitor can match. Uncertainty: zero Kano e-grocery demand data exists. Next action: Days 1–30 validation plan; go/no-go on its thresholds.

2. Which customer segment first? Households in 2–3 core urban LGAs doing weekly stock-up (₦10k+ baskets) as the revenue base, simultaneously pursuing 3–5 institutional buyers (caterers/offices/schools) as the density shortcut — bulk, prepaid, no consumer CAC. Students (BUK) only via batched campus drops once routing works. Evidence: baskets ₦10k+ are the only contribution-positive zone (Deliverable 6); B2B has no public competition evidence but zero demand evidence either → 5 interviews in week 1 (A1).

3. Which initial products? 60–100 ambient SKUs: staples and grains (Dawanau), oils, confectionery/biscuits (Ghadco/Mamuda), beverages incl. Pop Cola (NBC Challawa/Ghadco), detergent/household (Too Clean, OK Plast), shelf-stable juices. No chilled/frozen in phase 1 (no cold chain: <1,000 reefers nationally). Include items where OurOwn can beat the market index, exclude price-transparent branded items that only clear 3–5% unless they drive basket size. Next action: Sheet 1 margin table from real quotes (A3).

4. How to source? Three-lane: (a) direct from Ghadco/Mamuda/NBC depot for Kano-made goods (freight ≈ 0); (b) cash-and-carry at Singer/Dawanau for everything else, arbitraging daily; (c) never budget from published "distributor terms" — none exist. Buy the lower landed cost per SKU; written returns/expiry clauses on every supplier account. Detail: 05-supplier-research-pack.md.

5. What evidence before approaching large distributors for commercial terms? (1) 40+ SKU price benchmark vs market; (2) 100+ completed orders proving order frequency; (3) a legal entity/objects confirmation so you can sign; (4) a 90-day volume projection with real cohort data; (5) premises/invoice capability (CAC, VAT position). Distributors sell to routes, not ideas — bring evidence you are a route.

6. Can a 5% gross margin work? Not on the modelled assumptions. At 5%, contribution is negative for every ₦5k–₦10k basket and only reaches +₦150–₦300 at ₦20k–₦30k with batched delivery and a fee. 5% also funds nothing for fixed costs at pilot volumes. What must change: blended gross margin to 8–10% (category mix: local lines 10–15%, price-sensitive branded 5–8%), minimum basket ₦10,000, delivery fee ≥₦800 below ₦15,000, batching to ₦600/order. With those, contribution is +₦300–₦700/order — still demanding (break-even ≈ 1,071–2,500 orders/month at ₦750k fixed cost). Full arithmetic: Deliverable 6.

7. What order value makes delivery economically sensible? Single-drop: ≥₦15,000 (contribution turns positive at 8–10% with a ₦1,000 fee). Batched: ≥₦10,000 works (≈+₦300–₦500). Below ₦10,000 delivery is structurally loss-making regardless of fee shown. Hence the hard minimum basket ₦10,000.

8. Delivery: separate, embedded, or free-above-threshold? Hybrid: free above ₦15,000, visible ₦800–₦1,000 fee below it, minimum basket ₦10,000. Separately charged is transparent and FCCPC-safe; free-above-threshold rewards exactly the baskets that fund it (Chowdeck trained the market with a ₦3,000 bar); full embedding rejected — +10–12% price to recover delivery makes OurOwn visibly dearer than the market and duplicates the trust/compliance risk of an undisclosed charge. Never call delivery free if it is hidden above market price. Model: Deliverable 6 §3.

9. Which payment provider for the pilot, and on what evidence? Paystack or Monnify, chosen by measured AOV: Monnify if AOV <₦6,000 (no ₦100 flat fee; T+0 settlement), Paystack if ≥₦6,000 or marketplace is near (best docs, T+1 contractual, multi-split). Evidence required before choosing: completed scorecard (Deliverable 6 §4) including a live ₦5,000 settlement test to the Afrizen bank account and written answers on refund SLA/webhook retries. Paga and PayGate: no published pricing — RFP-only, not pilot-ready. Flutterwave: redundancy (2%+VAT is the most expensive local rate).

10. Which technology features are essential before accepting real customer orders? All 🔴 items in 08-technical-operational-readiness.md §1 — non-negotiable core: server-side payment verification + reconciliation, idempotency/duplicate-payment protection, stock decremented at order, order state machine with notifications, refund flow, RBAC+MFA+audit logs, tested backups, privacy notice/consent, hosted checkout (no card data), and the 10 acceptance tests passed. Everything else (marketplace module, subscriptions, advanced analytics) waits.

11. What should founders measure in the first 30 days? Daily: orders, AOV, contribution/order, failed payments, on-time %. Weekly: 14–30-day repeat rate, availability %, failed-delivery %, complaints/100 orders, cash in bank, inventory days, payment-method mix, source channel. Plus the plan-specific outputs: 30 interviews, 100-order micro-pilot cohort, real per-drop cost, 40-SKU price index. Dashboard: Deliverable 11 §7.

12. Under what conditions introduce third-party vendors? Gate (all must hold): contribution-positive for 3 consecutive months; 30-day repeat ≥35%; fulfilment SLA ≥90%; working capital ≥3 months; legal entity + objects for marketplace; PSP splits enabled (Paystack multi-split); vendor T&Cs + NAFDAC warranties + DPAs drafted. Market evidence: marketplaces survived where owned grocery died (Jumia) — but only after trust infrastructure existed. Introduce 3–5 invited vendors first, not open registration.

13. What must be true before expanding to Kaduna, Jigawa, Katsina? (1) Kano polygon contribution-positive at full cost for 3 consecutive months; (2) repeat ≥35% with no decay; (3) supply spine confirmed in the new city (distributor or haulage cost per unit benchmarked); (4) dedicated local operator (not remote management); (5) 3 months of new-city working capital without debt; (6) delivery partner SLA verified on the ground. Alerzo rule: profitable-in-two-cities was the beginning of its collapse, not proof of scale.

14. What must be true before Abuja? Same as Q13, plus independent verification of the founders' claimed delivery partner (coverage, pricing, SLA in writing — currently an unverified claim), and Abuja's different economics (higher basket, higher rent, established competition from Glovo/Chowdeck/PricePally). Abuja is a competitor market, Kano is a white-space market — the entry logic differs; do not expand to "match" it before Kano is a machine.

15. What evidence justifies the ₦60m/₦100m targets? ₦60m = 500 orders/month at ₦10k (17/day); ₦100m = 833/month (28/day). Justification requires: measured AOV ≥₦10k, measured 30-day repeat ≥30%, acquisition channel delivering ≥28 orders/day at CAC below contribution, and delivery capacity at that volume with ≥85% on-time. If the 30-day pilot shows ≥5/day sustained with positive contribution and repeat ≥30%, the targets become credible scaling arithmetic; without that data they are slogans. Remember: ₦100m sales ≈ ₦3–8m contribution at modelled inputs — not ₦100m profit.

16. Three biggest reasons the business could fail? 1. Order-density shortfall — positive per-order economics, insufficient orders per km² (GoLemon's exact death; Copia's). 2. Margin collapse under real costs — 5% gross margin + single-drop delivery + small baskets + failed deliveries = negative contribution; compounded by food inflation (19.59%) outrunning prices. 3. Trust failure at first contact — an unreconciled payment, a failed delivery, or a spoiled substitution in a market where e-commerce is the #1 fraud channel kills word-of-mouth, which is the only affordable acquisition channel in Kano. (Runners-up: funding run-out forcing debt (Alerzo); premature multi-city expansion.)

17. What should founders do in the next seven days? 1. Day 1: decide the pilot polygon and write the operating policy (min basket ₦10k, fee rule, batch windows, prepaid-first) — one page, binding. 2. Days 1–3: book supplier meetings — Ghadco (46 Tafawa Balewa Rd, 0916 255 5650), Mamuda distributor line, NBC Challawa; prepare the 40-SKU quote sheet. 3. Days 2–5: run 10 customer interviews (script in Deliverable 11 §A1) — behaviour, live-order ask. 4. Days 3–5: mystery-shop delivery: GIG Kano's 3 centres, Fez Kano hub, 3 local dispatchers — one identical brief, get per-drop quotes. 5. Days 3–7: Paystack + Monnify sandbox, ₦5,000 live settlement test each; written questions on refunds/webhooks. 6. Days 1–7 (parallel): lawyer meeting with the eight questions (Deliverable 9 §10); independent technical review kickoff (Deliverable 8 §4). 7. Day 7: score everything against the Day-7 checkpoint in Deliverable 11 — continue, modify, or stop. Do not buy inventory before Day 7.

Every recommendation above is only as good as the evidence cited in Deliverables 1–12. The three most load-bearing inputs — Kano AOV, Kano delivery cost, Kano repeat rate — are all unmeasured; the 30-day plan exists to measure them before capital is committed.
